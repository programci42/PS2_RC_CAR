Running a PS2 Slim Safely from a Battery and Powering the Relay Loads

Prepared: 21 July 2026 | Target system: PS2 Slim with external adapter, Seda Elektronik 8-relay board, 4 DC motors, fan and lamp

Short conclusion: The most sensible and affordable solution is a 12 V 7 Ah sealed lead-acid battery, or on a better budget a 12.8 V LiFePO4 battery, with a separate buck regulator producing 8.50 V DC for the PS2 Slim. Motors, fan and lamp must never be fed from the PS2 USB port or from the PS2's 8.5 V supply line; they must run from a separately fused 12 V load line through the relay contacts.
What about the PS2's own adapter? When running from a battery we do not use the PS2's original mains adapter. Its job is done by the DC-DC regulator on the battery side: the ~12 V from the battery is dropped to 8.50 V DC and fed to the PS2 Slim's normal adapter input. If you want to use it from the wall socket at home you can still use the original adapter; never connect it in parallel with the battery circuit at the same time.
Current installed setup (September 2026): A single 3S 11.1 V 40C LiPo is split into two branches. One branch is dropped to 8.50 V with an XL4015 and goes to the PS2 Slim DC input; the other goes directly to the four RS-540 motors through the relay contacts. The Seda Elektronik 8-channel relay board's separate 5 V power USB and HID data USB occupy the PS2 Slim's two USB ports. The two left motors are in parallel, the two right motors in parallel; the first four SPDT relays set direction.

Recommended Power Architecture

Two separate power lines are the cleanest solution for this project:

Line Feeds Why separate?
PS2 line PS2 Slim and the data side of the USB relay board The PS2 wants a stable, clean 8.5 V DC. Motor noise or a sudden current dip can lock up the PS2.
Load line 4 DC motors, fan, lamp Motors draw high current at start-up. These loads must be fed separately through a fuse and the relay contacts. 
12V battery / 12.8V LiFePO4
   |
   +-- 7.5A fuse -- main switch -- XL4016 buck -- 8.50V -- PS2 Slim DC input
   |
   +-- separate fuse box -- relay contacts -- motors / fan / lamp
Important: The PS2 Slim's USB ports are for sending data to the relay board and powering small electronics. Feeding 4 motors, a fan or a lamp from the PS2 USB port is not correct.

Technical Basis

On PS2 Slim models with an external adapter the typical adapter rating is 8.5 V DC, about 5.65 A. This means the PS2 side should be planned for a capacity of roughly 48 W.

The common approach in portable PS2 projects is to drop the ~11.1 V / 12 V from the battery to the 8.5 V the PS2 wants with a quality DC-DC regulator. PowerStream's car adapter for the PS2 also produces 8.5 V from a 10–15 V input, which shows this architecture is on the right track.

Power and Run-Time Calculation

Item Calculation / Note Practical result
PS2 Slim 8.5 V × 5.65 A adapter capacity is about 48 W. Real consumption varies with the game, the disc and the USB load. Choose a regulator of at least 6 A, preferably above 8 A.
12 V 7 Ah sealed lead-acid Nominal energy about 84 Wh. In practice not all of it is usable with lead-acid. For the PS2 alone, about 1–1.5 hours is realistic.
12.8 V 12 Ah LiFePO4 Nominal energy about 154 Wh. More efficient and lighter than lead-acid. For the PS2 alone, about 2.5–3 hours can be expected.
Motors, fan, lamp These loads determine the total run time. Motor start-up current can be far higher than running current. No precise run time can be calculated without measuring the motors' current.

Can a Ready-Made LiPo / Li-ion Pack Be Used?

Ready-made LiPo packs are left off the main list not because they are unusable, but because for this project they are not as plug-and-play and forgiving as lead-acid or LiFePO4. RC-type 3S LiPo packs can deliver high current, which is an advantage for motors. But the risks with wrong charging, over-discharge, short circuit or mechanical damage are more serious.

Option Advantage Watch out for
3S LiPo 11.1 V Light, high current, strong in motorised systems. Needs a balance charger, a low-voltage alarm/cut-off and a safe storage bag.
Li-ion pack with BMS More controlled in daily use than LiPo; the BMS can provide protection. The pack's continuous output current must cover PS2 and motor loads. Cheap packs may have a low BMS current limit.
LiFePO4 pack Safer chemistry, long cycle life, compatible with 12 V systems. Higher initial cost, needs a suitable LiFePO4 charger.

My ranking: LiFePO4 is the cleanest solution; on a low budget the 12 V 7 Ah lead-acid; if weight is critical and you are willing to manage battery safety, 3S LiPo. If you use LiPo, the PS2 line still must not connect directly to the pack; a buck regulator must produce 8.50 V.

Wiring Plan

  1. Put a main fuse on the battery output.
  2. After the fuse, put the main on/off switch.
  3. Run the PS2 line through its own fuse to the XL4016 buck regulator.
  4. Set the XL4016 output to 8.50 V with a multimeter before connecting the PS2.
  5. Check the regulator's output polarity: plus and minus must not be reversed.
  6. Build a separate fused 12 V line for motors, fan and lamp.
  7. Use the Seda relay board's contacts to switch the motor/fan/lamp supply line.
  8. Because the motor polarity is reversed, do not fit a unidirectional flyback diode across the terminals. For noise suppression use a bidirectional TVS or an RC snubber suited to bidirectional operation.
  9. For the first test, try the regulator output with a load resistor or lamp instead of the PS2.
  10. If everything checks out, connect the PS2 and test without motors first.

Can a Power Bank Be Used?

A standard 5 V power bank is not directly suitable. The PS2 wants about 8.5 V and can draw high current. A powerful USB-C PD power bank with a PD trigger giving 12 V or 15 V into the buck regulator can be tried for the PS2 alone. But once motors, fan and lamp are in the same system, the power bank solution becomes more expensive and more troublesome.

My recommendation: Use a 12 V lead-acid or LiFePO4 battery instead of a power bank for this project. A power bank only makes sense if portability is paramount and the motor loads run from a separate battery.

Safety Notes

  • Never feed 12 V directly into the PS2 input; the target for the PS2 Slim is 8.50 V DC.
  • Measure the regulator output with a multimeter before connecting it to the PS2.
  • Fuse the PS2 line and the motor line separately.
  • Motor start-up current is high, so check the relay contact current ratings.
  • DC motors cause sparking and noise at relay contacts; use a diode/TVS/snubber as appropriate.
  • A battery short circuit carries a serious heat and fire risk; leave no bare cables.
  • If you build a Li-ion pack, always use a BMS. Never connect random 18650 cells directly.
  • In the first trials, test with only the PS2 and the relay board, without connecting motors.
